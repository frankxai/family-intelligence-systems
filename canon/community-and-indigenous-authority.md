# Community and Indigenous Authority

Family consent is not always the only authority that matters. Cultural knowledge may be held collectively and may remain governed by a community, clan, nation, religious body, language group, or appointed custodian even when a copy is possessed by an individual family member.

The Family Intelligence System adopts the people- and purpose-oriented direction of the CARE Principles—Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, and Ethics—and supports Local Contexts-style community-defined conditions.

## Rules

1. Individual consent cannot override a valid collective or community restriction.
2. Public availability, copyright expiry, physical possession, or archival access does not automatically authorize digitization, AI processing, indexing, translation, derivative creation, federation, or publication.
3. Community authorities may require attribution, restricted audiences, gender or seasonal access, no AI, no indexing, no derivatives, no federation, repatriation, suppression, or complete non-digitization.
4. The system stores an opaque authority reference and decision metadata; sensitive community identity details remain in the authorized tenant system.
5. Unknown or disputed authority blocks publication, federation, AI transformation, and public indexing pending human review.
6. Revocation or repatriation triggers dependency discovery across originals, derivatives, caches, exports, indexes, and recipients.

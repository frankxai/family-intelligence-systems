# Identity and account recovery protocol v1

Relationship is not authentication. A person receives access through a verified personal account, a family membership, and resource-level authorization.

## Account baseline

- Prefer passkeys with a second registered device or hardware-backed credential.
- Give every adult a personal account; never normalize a shared household password.
- Bind sessions to `actor_id`, `family_id`, role, authentication time, and recovery state.
- Require re-authentication for export, publication, guardian changes, recovery, and succession actions.
- Keep service accounts connector-scoped and unable to inherit human authority.

## Invitation

1. A steward chooses a role and initial circle without granting blanket resource access.
2. The system sends a single-use, expiring link through a verified channel after human confirmation.
3. The invited person sees the family, controller, requested role, privacy notice, and decline option before account creation.
4. Acceptance creates an audit event and no public profile.

## Recovery

- Recovery codes remain with the person, outside the family database.
- Social recovery uses designated humans, delay, notification, and revocation—not one administrator silently resetting an account.
- A recovered account receives a restricted session until sensitive actions are re-verified.
- Loss of access never transfers ownership, consent, or succession authority.

## Children and age transition

A guardian-managed child profile is not an adult account. At the configured age and jurisdictional milestone, the system runs an age-transition review: explain stored data, allow correction/deletion, issue a personal account, and request fresh consent. Parent or guardian publication approval never silently becomes the young person's adult consent.

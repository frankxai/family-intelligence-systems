# Consent Protocol v1

## Principles

Consent is specific, informed, granular, revocable, and attributable. One receipt cannot silently authorize unrelated purposes.

Each receipt records:

- the affected person and authorizing person
- authority basis, including guardian authority when applicable
- exact purpose and data categories
- allowed audience/scope and actions
- grant, expiry, withdrawal, and version timestamps
- notice version and language
- evidence of the interaction without storing unnecessary sensitive text

## Living people

No living person is public by default. A family steward cannot consent to public publication on behalf of a capable adult merely because they are related.

## Children

Child data is critical. A guardian receipt is purpose-limited, the child receives age-appropriate information, and the system requires a re-consent/transition process when the child gains independent authority. Names, schools, exact dates, locations, faces, health, behavior, and private learning profiles are excluded from public samples.

## Withdrawal

Withdrawal blocks future processing for that consent purpose and queues review of derived/shared artifacts. The audit trail may retain minimal integrity metadata while payloads remain cryptographically deletable.

## Revocation procedure

Revocation is a first-class request, not an account preference. The system must:

1. receive and timestamp the request without requiring the requester to identify every copy of their data;
2. verify the requester or their documented authority without asking them to resubmit the withdrawn data;
3. stop future actions covered by the receipt as soon as the request is verified;
4. inventory affected claims, evidence, transcriptions, summaries, search indexes, exports, public artifacts, and delegated agent memories;
5. suppress or withdraw affected public artifacts before debating non-essential follow-up work;
6. create a human-review task for shared facts, another person's rights, legal retention, or an already-delivered export;
7. record what was stopped, withdrawn, retained only as minimal integrity metadata, or could not be recalled from a recipient-controlled copy; and
8. issue a revocation receipt to the authorized requester without exposing other family members' data.

The system may retain minimum event metadata needed to prove that a consent state changed. It must not treat audit retention as permission to keep source payloads, embeddings, full-text indexes, or generated summaries available.

## Derived and external artifacts

Every derived artifact must retain source and consent references so revocation can be evaluated. Examples include a transcript, translation, OCR output, timeline card, biography draft, vector index entry, family export, and public page.

Revocation cannot promise recall of a recipient-controlled export, an independently archived public copy, or a message already received outside the system. In those cases the system records the limitation, stops further sharing, withdraws first-party access where possible, and offers a human review path.

## Authority conflicts

A capable adult's withdrawal for their own data takes precedence over a steward's convenience. A guardian's authority for a child is purpose-limited and reviewed when the child reaches the configured age-of-authority threshold. Conflicting or uncertain authority moves to `review_required`; an agent may not resolve it.

An unverified message may open a `pending` request but may not execute a withdrawal, delete data, issue a publication takedown, or obtain confirmation about protected artifacts. A human Guardian may create a temporary, auditable safety hold when there is a credible imminent-risk report; that hold is not a completed withdrawal and receives expedited authority review.

## Receipt-state reconciliation

The withdrawal request, the prior consent receipt, and the audit event are one state transition. An implementation may record `executed` or `partially_executed` only after authority verification, a completed artifact inventory, and synchronization of the prior consent receipt. The inventory includes at least the consent receipt and each known source, derivative, public page, index, export, or external-copy limitation. A complete inventory may conclude that no retained artifacts exist; it must still record that conclusion.

- when the withdrawal removes all remaining coverage, mark the receipt `withdrawn` and set `withdrawn_at` to the effective time;
- when limited remaining coverage still exists, mark the old receipt `superseded` and create a successor receipt limited to the remaining purpose and actions, linked through `supersedes`;
- when authority, scope, an artifact inventory, or an affected person's rights remain uncertain, keep the withdrawal in review and do not report execution as complete.

A `partially_executed` withdrawal has a complete inventory and a settled consent state, but lists the remaining recipient-controlled or otherwise unresolved artifact references. It is not a substitute for `review_required`.

The successor record represents the unwithdrawn remainder of documented permission; it must not broaden purpose, scope, action, or audience beyond the earlier receipt.

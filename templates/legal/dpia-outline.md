# Family Intelligence DPIA outline

This is a planning template, not legal advice. Complete it with qualified privacy and legal review before hosted processing of real family or child data.

## Processing description

- Controller, processor, hosting regions, subprocessors
- Family, household, public-archive, and advisor scopes
- Intake, transcription, search, agent/tool calls, publication, export, deletion, and succession flows
- Categories of people, including children, elders, vulnerable people, and non-users mentioned by relatives
- Data categories, special-category data, inferred relationships, and source artifacts

## Necessity and proportionality

- Purpose for each data category
- Lawful basis by processing purpose and jurisdiction
- Why a smaller scope or less data cannot meet the purpose
- Retention, correction, contest, withdrawal, deletion, and portability
- Safeguards against function creep, advertising, data brokerage, and model training

## Risk scenarios

- False or disputed parentage/adoption inference
- Exposure of a living person, child, location, health condition, religion, ethnicity, migration history, abuse allegation, finance, or property
- Malicious relative, coercive guardian, account takeover, prompt injection, unsafe connector, or insider access
- Irreversible publication, failed deletion, failed restore, or incorrect succession release
- Re-identification through family graph combinations

## Controls and evidence

- Per-person identity and family-scoped authorization
- Claims-not-facts lifecycle and human steward authority
- Purpose-specific consent receipts and public redaction preview
- Child and age-transition protections
- Encryption, object quarantine, malware scanning, RLS, audit, key separation, and deletion
- Connector/MCP allowlists, approval gates, outbound data logs, and model retention settings
- Complete encrypted export and tested restore
- Incident response, complaints, independent review, and regulator consultation decision

## Decision

Residual risk, control owner, test evidence, review date, legal sign-off, and whether prior supervisory-authority consultation is required.
